How to find the inner border on a full-art card

Separate the physical card edge from an inner reference line. Learn when full-art card centering can be measured and when a result would be a guess.

By JIAPENG Zhao · CenterMint developer
Updated September 9, 2026

The outer edge and the inner reference are different

The outer edge is the physical boundary of the card. The inner reference is a consistent transition in the print that lets you compare the surrounding border widths. Silver, gold, yellow and dark borders can all provide a reference; color alone does not decide where it lies.

A character, title banner, attack box or foil reflection can create a stronger line than the actual border. Choosing that line can move the top or bottom inner frame far into the artwork.

A practical four-side check

  1. Locate the physical card edge without including the sleeve, case or background.
  2. Follow the border-to-print transition at several clear positions along each side. Avoid rounded corners and highlights.
  3. Check that all four selected lines describe the same kind of reference. A line above a title is not interchangeable with the bottom edge of that title box.
  4. Use the loupe to compare uncertain positions with the original image. Improve the photo if the transition is obscured.

Why a fixed inset cannot be the answer

A plausible inset is useful for narrowing a search area, but forcing equal distances assumes the answer before measuring. Print alignment, cutting and design families vary. A correct result must preserve real imbalance instead of automatically centering the inner frame.

Illustration: if the left and right border widths are 3 mm and 2 mm, replacing both with 2.5 mm would report 50/50 and erase the real 60/40 measurement.

When there is no measurable inner frame

Some borderless or full-art designs do not have a single continuous inner reference on all four sides. Do not treat an arbitrary rectangle or a default 50/50 as a measured result. A matching official design reference may help interpretation, but alignment to a template would need its own validation.

If only two sides are clear, that does not establish the other axis. Record the uncertain sides separately or use a clearer card side. Reflective and sleeved cards may require another photograph.

What we can claim

CenterMint combines automatic frame suggestions with manual edge control and a focus loupe. These controls help inspect and correct a result; they do not prove every color or full-art design is detected accurately. Demonstrations and generated sample cards are not substitutes for an independent labeled photo test.

This guide describes a measurement method, not an official grading policy. Published scales such as CGC's distinguish card categories; do not apply a sports-card threshold to every TCG card.
